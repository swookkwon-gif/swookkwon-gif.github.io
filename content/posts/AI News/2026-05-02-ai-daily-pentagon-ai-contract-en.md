## 1. Pentagon Signs AI Deployment Contracts for Classified Networks with Major AI Firms (Excluding Anthropic)
The US Department of Defense has signed contracts with 7 major AI companies, including SpaceX, OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, and Oracle, to deploy AI capabilities on classified networks. These agreements aim to transform the US military into an "AI-first" fighting force and enhance decision-making dominance across all warfighting domains. Notably, Anthropic was excluded from the contracts due to disagreements over AI safety guidelines, highlighting the tension between ethical considerations in AI development and the government's demand for control.

## 4. AI Hardware Innovation and Supply Chain Strengthening: Kyocera Develops Advanced Semiconductor Substrates
Kyocera announced the commercialization of a new multilayer ceramic core substrate for advanced semiconductor packaging, such as xPUs and switch ASICs. Built with proprietary fine ceramic materials for high-density wiring and exceptional rigidity, this new product drastically reduces warpage in high-performance semiconductor packages. Meanwhile, Apple discontinued the cheapest Mac Mini model (256GB) and raised the starting price to $799, driven by a surge in demand from engineers looking to run agentic AI tools locally, illustrating how AI infrastructure demand is impacting the consumer desktop market.

## 5. Strengthening Defenses Against AI Security Threats
Researchers at OX Security discovered a critical command execution vulnerability in the STDIO transport of MCP—the default connection for local tools in AI agents—affecting approximately 200,000 servers. In response to such threats, Anthropic launched the public beta of Claude Security for Enterprise customers, utilizing Claude Opus 4.7 to scan for code vulnerabilities and recommend patches. Additionally, security researchers at Theori demonstrated the potential of LLM-based code auditing tools by finding a critical vulnerability (CVE-2026-31431) in the Linux kernel in just one hour using the AI tool Xint Code AI.

## 6. AI Cost Efficiency and Shifting Business Models
Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI budget in just four months, primarily spending it on Anthropic's Claude Code. This highlights remarkable productivity gains from AI—with 95% of engineers using AI tools and 70% of committed code being AI-generated—but simultaneously raises questions regarding the cost-efficiency and business models of AI services. Moves such as GitHub Copilot shifting to 'usage-based billing' and Anthropic's Claude Code raising projected costs and signaling a shift toward a pay-as-you-go structure indicate that AI service business models are transitioning from subscription-based to usage-based.
